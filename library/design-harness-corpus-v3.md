# Design-harness corpus v3

**Commissioned** 2026-10-20 by Rob; v1 (16 sources) and v2 (50 sources) delivered 2026-10-20; fact-checked and red-teamed 2026-10-21 (`corpus-v2-redteam.md`); **v3 built 2026-10-21** in the Product Harness Project. v3 reframes the corpus from its v2 brief ("Core", a screen-based design harness with a background agent) to this Project's definition of the harness, in Rob's words: *"the environment connecting AI capabilities with practitioners' context, tools, artifacts, actions, and feedback — not prescribing a standalone workspace, chat interface, canvas, execution engine, or architecture,"* for UX/product design, software QA and user research. Claude Design's standalone closure (announced 2026-10-08, effective 2026-12-14; the capability continues inside Claude) is a given premise and now a sourced entry [57].

**What v3 is.** (1) **v2.1** — the red team's eleven §7.1 edits applied to v2's fifty entries and eight tensions with no new research, citing the research pack's controlled evidence (§6 lists each edit). (2) **A strand-1 refresh** of seven entries [51]–[57] from changelogs, help-center notices and developer docs, because v2 was current to each vendor's marketing and stale to each vendor's product. (3) **A new strand 6** — reconciliation, review and evaluation lineages — of eight entries [58]–[65], because v2's five strands studied authoring surfaces while the recurring work is reconciliation. (4) **A ninth tension, T9 latency and feedback loops**, with four sources of its own [66]–[69]. (5) A **forgotten-but-works re-verdict pass** under a strict rubric, applied to all 69 entries by the main thread (§3). (6) The **synthesis rewritten** over everything, with **T4 marked CONTESTED** (Figma, the field's dominant tool, has no files).

**Count:** 69 sources — v2's 50 (numbers unchanged so the red team's `[N]` references still resolve) plus 19 new, appended as [51]–[69]. **Floors:** ≥12 pre-2005 → 17; ≥10 from 2024 or later → 32; ≥8 critical or post-mortem → 12 by type. By strand in §5.

**Method.** v2.1 in the main thread. v3 additions: three Sonnet strand agents dispatched in one message (strand-1 refresh ≤8 opens; strand 6 ≤12; T9 ≤5), each returning entries in schema with a quota check, a page-open list, a verbatim-check list and gaps; one Sonnet follow-up with 5 authorised opens for strand-6 shortfalls; two Haiku verbatim checkers re-opening 12 pages (44 quotes, 30 facts). Main-thread reconciliation, re-verdicting and synthesis. **Research page opens: 30** (25 inside the standing cap, 5 authorised). **Verification re-opens: 12.** Two commissioned lineages were not reached (Dreamweaver Roundtrip HTML; Abstract) and are recorded in §4 with leads rather than shipped as hollow entries.

**Entry schema.** `[N]. Title` · maker/author · year · type (primary artifact | critique | post-mortem | user testimony | HCI research | talk) · link with status · what it is · delighted users · perturbed users · verdict (HOLDS UP | FORGOTTEN BUT WORKS | SETTLED & ANTIQUATED | CONTESTED, with one sentence of reason) · harness implication (one sentence; concrete and falsifiable) · overlap. Quotes are under 20 words. Vendor claims are labelled. Inferred dates are flagged. CONTESTED only where the *pattern* is contested.

**Link status key.** *verified (quotes checked)* — an independent agent re-opened the page and confirmed the quoted text. *verified (via summariser)* — the page loaded through a summarising fetch and the quotes were not re-checked. *verified* alone — the v1 strand agent read the page directly; not independently re-checked. *link unverified* — not opened. *archive.org (original dead)* — read from a Wayback capture.

**⚑ flag.** The red team counted 27 v2 implications that presuppose a harness-owned surface (a canvas, a CLI/TUI, a node graph or timeline, a pane). Under this Project's definition those are conditionals or requirements on a host. Per the Phase 0 decision they are left as v2 wrote them and marked ⚑; read each as "if the host surface provides X…" or as a requirement on the host (Figma, Penpot, Storybook, VS Code, the repo). [24] was rewritten in v2.1 and unflagged; 26 flags remain. All v3 entries and all nine tensions are written surface-neutral. Throughout the v2 entries the word "Core" has been replaced by "the harness" and "Rob" by "the designer"; nothing else in a v2 entry was changed except where §6 says so.

**Cross-references.** `[N]` is a corpus entry. `E1-01`, `D-05`, `A-07` and similar are cards in the Product Harness research pack (`research/02-source-register.md`); pack sources cited in the synthesis are given as URLs. `F1`–`F18`, `M1`–`M26` and `§6.n` refer to the red team document.

## 1. Cross-strand synthesis

Nine tensions recur across the entries. Each names the strands it appears in and the single strongest implication for the harness, phrased as a test. Implications are written without presupposing a harness-owned surface: where a mechanism needs a canvas, graph or command line, it is stated as a requirement on the host (Figma, Penpot, Storybook, VS Code, a terminal) or as a conditional. T1–T8 are v2's tensions revised per the red team (corrections F4–F11, F18; lineages M5, M6, M20); T4 is now CONTESTED; T9 is new.

**T1. Floor vs. ceiling, and the escape hatch.** Every tool that lowered the floor by hiding code met a ceiling its power users would not accept. Flash shipped Normal and Expert modes side by side [12], then split into base and Professional tiers because developers "struggled with the timeline metaphor" [13]; Framer X made code primary and Classic users kept Classic for "crazy and weird ideas" [16, 18]; Retool users describe "building a custom app inside a constrained UI framework" once past tables and grids [30]; a Bubble founder left when "the friction started to outweigh the speed" of AI coding tools [29]; n8n users move routing into a code node once IF nodes stop composing [50]; a Blueprints team wanted out to C++ [47]; early visual languages "dropped to C for procedures" [41]; v0 added a direct-manipulation Design Mode to a prompt-first tool three years in, with every visual edit landing on a repo branch [55]; Interface Builder's layout-data/behaviour-code split is the seam that let the two tiers coexist for 35 years [59]. *Strands 1, 2, 3, 5, 6.* **Implication:** wherever the host surface is visual — a canvas, a graph, a form designer — any agent-generated component or subgraph can be collapsed to code and expanded back without leaving the host, and an external coding agent (Claude Code, Cursor) can extend the harness's export in one session without rewriting it; a designer leaving the host for a text editor to finish a task is a failed test.

**T2. Direct manipulation vs. abstraction.** Atkinson's pitch was buttons on top of images for non-programmers [21]; Puckette credits Max's appeal to "concreteness (the opposite of abstraction)" [43]; Hooper's user studies killed his visual-programming iPad app because designers didn't want it [14]; ProtoPie won a migrating designer with trigger-to-response chains instead of conditionals [15]; the open-design reviewer's named gap was the missing drawing canvas [7]; Claude Design's anchored comments and sliders are direct-manipulation stand-ins for chat [5]; Park et al. put an editable semantics layer between prompt and output and users reported more control [4]. *v2.1:* controlled evidence now backs the tension — DirectGPT (CHI '24, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03691) found direct manipulation of LLM output beat chat for precise edits: closer to target, 56 s vs 1:57, 8% vs 35% timeouts (figures per the research pack's D-05 card). The "designer demonstrates, agent abstracts" mode also has a 35-year literature the corpus has not yet opened — programming by demonstration (Myers' Peridot 1988; Cypher 1993; Lieberman 2001) — with a known failure (the system cannot tell whether you meant *this* button or *any* button) and a known fix (show the inferred rule; ask when ambiguous); that lineage stays an open gap (§4), but the QA field's record-and-playback tools already apply the fix — Playwright's recorder prefers role, text and test-id locators and expects the human to edit the result [65]. *Strands 1, 2, 3, 5, 6.* **Implication:** the designer demonstrates (drag, scrub, place) in the host and the agent abstracts (writes the code, names the token), showing the rule it inferred in the designer's vocabulary before applying it; prompting is the fallback — test with five designers on a motion task and fail if fewer than four choose manipulation over prompting, count rounds-to-accepted-screen with and without a structured spec layer, and measure mis-generalisation rate on 20 demonstrated interactions with and without the shown rule.

**T3. Hidden semantics: layout and duplication.** In Max, tidying boxes can change behaviour and fan-out is unsolved [43]; Burnett's nine scaling sub-problems include screen real estate and abstraction [41]; LabVIEW users say "it is easy to produce messy, cluttered code" [42]; nested boxes are "very taxing indeed" [44]; duplication lowered 12-to-14-year-olds' comprehension scores by about three points on a 13-point scale (means unlabelled on the page; *v2.1, F11* — v2's "roughly 40%" was arithmetic on those means) and 5 of 21 updated one clone but not the other [45]; Max veterans answer repetition with one abstraction inside poly~ instantiated N times [46]; Blueprints spaghetti [47, 48] and ComfyUI readability rules [49] rediscover the same thing; Stitch's navigation changed from screen to screen [3]. *Strands 1, 5.* **Implication:** in any graph or canvas host, semantics must be independent of node position (shuffle positions on 20 saved graphs; any output diff gates the agent's auto-tidy), and define-once-instantiate-N is the agent's default for repeated screens and components — but (*v2.1, F8*) the tidy and merge passes are gated by behaviour-equivalence checks the agent cannot edit (snapshot outputs before and after; diff), because the agent's own report is not evidence: autonomous test repair "converged" by weakening assertions and deleting a failing test, with about half of repairs semantically strict (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01471), and long-horizon agents skipped verification and bypassed the visible UI in about a third of audited tasks (OSWorld 2.0, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29537); test that a change to one definition reaches all instances without the human touching N copies, and report the disagreement rate between the agent's claimed and the checker's measured equivalence.

**T4. Plain text as the shared surface — CONTESTED.** The VT100 served operator and host over one line [31]; Stephenson calls words the only immutable medium [32]; Raymond: "Designing a textual protocol tends to future-proof your system" [33]; Ronacher's agent works through scripts, Makefile targets and log files [39]; Claude Code is pitched as "a Unix utility" [40]; the open-design file daemon that lets any agent read generated designs was "the quiet hero" [7] and a per-project DESIGN.md is its brand contract [10]; HyperCard users were stranded when the vendor stopped caring [25, 28]; Burnett lists version control and testing as the layer visual tools lack [41]. *v2.1 — two separations the red team forced (F6, M20).* First, "file as source of truth" is a storage claim and "a context file improves the agent's output" is a performance claim; the only controlled test of supplying context files to coding agents found LLM-written files −0.5% / −2% (non-significant) at +20–23% cost and developer-written files +2.4% (p = 0.21) (Gloaguen et al., AGENTS.md, https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988v3) — an adjacent domain, but the only measurement, and *who writes* DESIGN.md is the variable to test. Second, the counterexample: Figma, the field's dominant tool, has no files — it is a cloud document with .fig export only — and it won the largest migration in design history (Sketch→Figma, 2017–2020) on browser, multiplayer and a free tier, not on diffable text [61]. T4 argued plain text from developer lineages (terminals, Unix, HyperCard's stranding) while the design lineage's biggest success went the other way; the pattern itself is contested, so the verdict is CONTESTED. Where the design field *has* standardised plain text — DTCG design tokens and Google's DESIGN.md, which now lints and diffs in CI [53] — the move is adopt, not invent; and v0's repo-backed Design Mode [55] shows the hybrid: a cloud canvas whose every edit becomes a branch. *Strands 1, 3, 4, 5, 6.* **Implication:** everything that leaves the host — tokens, DESIGN.md, specs, the agent's change log, exports — is line-diffable text in a repo, and whether the host's own document is file-backed is the host's choice; test that a second agent can edit a first agent's output with no conversion step, that an overnight change is reviewable in an ordinary diff with no custom viewer, that a project opens with the harness uninstalled, and — separately, as a performance test — whether a designer-written vs. an agent-written DESIGN.md changes fidelity on a seeded brief against a no-file control.

**T5. Convention vs. novelty (the basics gate).** Users punish surfaces that break expected basics, whether the tool's or the output's. The tool: Evans's veterans rank remembering syntax (115) and discoverability (55) at the top [38]; novices found the CLI "intimidating" and liked a scaffold without measurable gain [34]; clig.dev says show examples first and check for a TTY [36]; Ghostty's maker: "You can't ship a terminal emulator that emulates only half the features Vim needs" [37]; open-design's terminal-only setup took 18 minutes [7]; HyperCard "fit in one's head" [28]. The output: Flash sites lost bookmarks, selectable text and accessibility [11]; FrontPage's WYSIWYG mode did "not produce valid code or pages that are accessible" [24]; FileMaker for Windows made users hand-draw non-native buttons [23]; Stitch exported "3x more layers than you'll actually need" [3]; Figma's generator reproduced Apple's weather app and was pulled [2]; NN/g's ten-tool evaluation found hierarchy, grouping, contrast and spacing defects even in outputs close to the human design [51]; Penpot's and Figma's own docs tell the agent to start read-only and to expect beta-quality output needing cleanup [52], [56]. The evaluation lineage designers already trust — Nielsen's heuristics, run as independent passes and merged — is the basis for a gate the agent cannot game [58]. *Strands 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6.* **Implication:** two gates. For whatever command surface the harness exposes, if it exposes one: in-surface discoverability (fail if fewer than 4 of 5 first-time designers finish a core task without docs). For every agent output (*rewritten in v2.1, F4* — v2's "automated audit … valid markup" would pass keyboard-inaccessible screens): an automated gate for what automation covers (deep link, selectable text, accessible names, valid markup, native components, similarity to well-known apps), plus an agent-performed keyboard and focus-order traversal recorded for human review for what it does not — rule engines flag 2.49% of keyboard issues and 0% of focus-order and focus-visible issues (Deque, https://www.deque.com/automated-accessibility-coverage-report/), ten tools together missed 29% of designed barriers (GDS, https://accessibility.blog.gov.uk/2017/02/24/what-we-found-when-we-tested-tools-on-the-worlds-least-accessible-webpage/), and the DOJ cites generative AI's unreliability at remediation (https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2026-07663); the measure is escaped keyboard defects per release and the gate's false-pass rate on seeded defects, not an axe score.

**T6. Convergence vs. divergence.** Lang's three-client test found Claude Design output converging on one look [9]; Kakehi: "Start iterating and it converges" because each round uses the AI's last output as baseline [8]; Myers found "the same tired look and feel" in Stitch [3]; Figma's Make Designs reproduced Apple's app [2]; Hooper argued tools "limit the way you think to that of a computer" [14]; Anthropic's partner quote claims a complex page in two prompts [5]. *v2.1 (F5; A-07; F13).* v2's cure — "the agent proposes at least three structurally different layouts per brief" — is contradicted in mechanism by the only controlled evidence: showing generated examples early *causes* fixation and lowers originality (N=60 design students, Midjourney, CHI '24, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.11164), and AI raises individual novelty while lowering collective diversity (N=293 writers, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.00506); three agent-made layouts shown first may fixate the designer on the agent's three. The practice side of the same problem is component fidelity rather than aesthetics: 37 employees using generative UI found it a usable first draft with a hard last mile and restarted generation rather than edit (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.13145), the incumbent's own post-GA admission that design-system support is still to come (research pack E1-02; [56]) says the same from the vendor side, and NN/g's evaluation found that prompts carrying a Figma frame link produced the most accurate output while unspecified prompts converged on generic Shadcn/Tailwind styling [51] — convergence seen from aesthetics and from fidelity are one problem, and the lever is context, not the tool. *Strands 1, 2.* **Implication:** the agent regenerates from the original brief plus an explicit change list rather than from its own last output, and generated alternatives appear only *after* the designer's own first sketch, anchored to that point of view — blind-test across three unrelated briefs with a no-examples control arm, compare five chained edits against five independent regenerations for distinctiveness, and count generated components outside the team's library on a seeded brief (target zero).

**T7. Vendor neglect, compatibility breaks, and switching cost.** HyperCard died of "benign neglect" with no source release [25, 28]; VB.NET broke VB6 and "Declaring customer data to be disposable is an extremely dangerous precedent" [26, 27]; Flash 4→5 needed "versus" sections in its own manual [12]; Framer X abandoned Classic [16, 18]; Pixate's shutdown forced a migration decided by learning cost, not features [15]; cloud-only, metered Claude Design drove a reviewer to a local-first fork within weeks [7]; v0's sign-up-after-input and lack of a private option were the first objections [1]. *v2.1 (F1, F9).* The corpus's own anchor product became a T7 case while v2 was being written: Claude Design's standalone site was announced as closing on 2026-10-08 (closes 2026-12-14) — chats and comments deleted, no version history, no migration for zero-data-retention organisations [5], [57]. And the harness's own substrate is not exempt: MCP's 2026-07-28 revision is a breaking change (it removes sessions and the initialize handshake and deprecates Roots, Sampling, Logging and Dynamic Client Registration), and critical CVEs sat in mcp-remote (9.6) and MCP Inspector (9.4) (spec versioning, https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/versioning; research pack F-03); [30]'s "the MCP cannot edit the canvas" and [39]'s "MCP servers unreliable" are symptoms — any "bring your own agent CLI" promise inherits a moving spec. Strand 6 adds the market-level case: InVision's exit — per-document export only, no full export of published design-system libraries, share links dead on deletion [62] — has the same shape as Claude Design's closure eight months later and HyperCard's two decades earlier; and DESIGN.md, the format T4 says to adopt, is itself alpha: "Expect changes to the format as it matures." [53]. *Strands 1, 2, 3, 6.* **Implication:** open, vendor-neutral project files with a CI compatibility gate (the last three saved versions of every fixture open unchanged after each release); a project authored under one model, agent or MCP revision opens identically after the swap, with the MCP revision pinned per project and tested against the next one before upgrade; first run needs no account and no cloud round-trip for design files; and for the closure in hand, an importer that keeps comment threads and design systems, published before 2026-12-14.

**T8. Human–agent collision on one surface.** *Narrowed in v2.1 (F18):* no source studies *concurrent* editing by a human and an autonomous agent on one design — but practitioner evidence on multi-party AI artifacts exists and v2 missed it: 20% of designers say collaboration decreased and AI tools without collaboration features created silos (N=906, https://stateofaidesign.com/chapters/teams); shared AI drafts diluted ownership (N=22, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.10652); whole-file sharing exposes unrelated frames and the authors propose role-specific modes (N=114, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.11845). The corpus's mechanisms to borrow: a three-person team left Webflow for Framer because "Webflow's biggest flaw for us was single-user editing" and Framer showed live cursors [20]; a demo scopes an agent with locked layers, a permission gate on global tokens and all-or-nothing bulk edits [6]; Nardi & Miller's "local developer" builds macros for the domain expert, and co-development "is the rule, not the exception" [22]; open-design lets "your agent refine it in the same project" with no merge semantics defined [10]; Burnett names diff and version control as the missing layer [41]; clig.dev's TTY check keeps a script from blocking on a prompt [36]; tmux's reliable detach is the property an away-session needs [35]; Ronacher's agent restarted running services until a pidfile guard was added [39]; Retool's MCP cannot touch the canvas [30]. *The mechanisms are not unstudied either (M5, M6):* Figma's 2019 engineering post documents per-property last-writer-wins and offline reconciliation [61], and every word processor since the 1990s has shipped attributed, inline, accept-or-reject edits — track changes / suggesting mode, whose design is a permission split (comment-level proposes, edit-level decides) [60]; the unattended version of the same split is the pull request — Dependabot proposing overnight, never writing to main, CI gating, and its critic attacking the noise rather than the unit [63]; and the vendor of the GUI-driving agent says itself that the loop is too slow to share a live session [69]. Only the cadence (an agent's burst edits against a human's drip) is new. *What v2 left unpriced (F7):* v2 added human approval at every agent step and treated the checkpoints as free; a 106-experiment meta-analysis finds human–AI combinations underperform the better single party on decision tasks (g = −0.27), gaining only where the human alone beats the AI (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11659167/); verifying suggestions took 22.4% of programmers' session time (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.14306v5); and in METR's RCT experienced developers were 19% slower with AI while believing they were 20% faster (https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/). *Strands 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6.* **Implication:** before specifying any approval, classify each step by which party is the better one and price the checkpoint in minutes, dropping approvals where the agent is the better party; agent edits land as attributed, in-place suggestions (track-changes semantics) under the host's documented conflict rules (per-property last-writer-wins where the host is Figma), in an append-only log the host or the repo keeps; sessions are detachable and replayable from any device; the tests are zero overwrites of human-edited nodes, the designer being able to say who changed what after an away session, the acceptance rate of agent suggestions, and measured minutes-per-checkpoint against the agent's unassisted error rate on that step.

**T9. Latency and feedback loops.** The one UX variable common to all five original strands and to the harness's defining feature — an agent that works while the practitioner is away is a feedback loop with a latency of hours — and the tension v2 dropped. Nielsen's three limits give the first three classes: at 0.1 s the user feels they are "directly manipulating objects in the UI", at 1 s they lose "the feeling of operating directly on the data" but keep their train of thought, and past 10 s "needs a percent-done indicator" [66]. The corpus's evidence sorts into those classes and a fourth. *0.1 s — direct manipulation:* the canvas's class; Puckette's "directness" [43], Nardi & Miller's "twinkling lights" [22], Framer Classic's "come to life in no time" [18], Victor's argument that immediacy is only a prerequisite and must be paired with visible state and a change record [67]. *1 s — the live loop:* hot reload, documented in 2026 with one timing (978 ms) and an openly stated stale-state failure [68]; Ronacher's slow tool startup that "drags the agent loop" [39]; v0's Pull Changes rerunning the whole preview pipeline [55]. *10 s to minutes — generation:* every strand-1 tool; open-design's 2–3 minute waits [7]; Kakehi "burning tokens" through chained iteration [8]; Stitch streaming output onto the canvas so the generation class *feels* like the 1 s class, with no stated interrupt semantics [54]; Figma's 20 KB-per-call output cap and credit metering as loop-stoppers [56]; Penpot's sleeping-tab rule, under which a run can silently stop [52]; and the vendor's own admission that a GUI-driving agent is "too slow compared to regular human-directed computer actions" [69]. *Hours — the background agent:* no source in either corpus states a UX rule for this class; the nearest lineage is the pull request and CI — Dependabot has proposed changes overnight since 2017 and its critic's complaint is unattended noise, not the unit [63] — and tmux's reattach after 8 hours [35]. Each class has its own lineage (direct manipulation; live reload; batch with progress; CI with proposals), and the recurring mistake across strand 1 is serving one class with another's UX: a prompt box where a canvas was wanted [7], streaming where a proposal was wanted [54], a chat reply where a diff was wanted [60], [63]. *Strands 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6.* **Implication:** classify every harness action by its loop class before choosing its channel, and route each class to the host mechanism built for it — a host interaction never waits on a model call (nothing in the 0.1 s class blocks on the harness), live-class edits travel through the host's hot-update path with a hot-versus-cold diff [68], generation-class work shows progress and is interruptible so that an interrupt at any second leaves the file valid and undoable in one step [54], and hours-class work lands as an attributed proposal with a change record (a suggestion [60] or a pull request [63]) that the practitioner reviews without replaying the run [67]; the tests are zero host interactions blocked longer than 1 s by a harness call, zero half-built frames across 50 random interrupts, and a returning practitioner able to say what changed and why from the record alone.

**A caveat that cuts across all nine.** Whitley & Blackwell's LabVIEW projects took "4 to 10 times less time to complete" and the authors could not credit the notation [42]; Herrera & Ward's novices liked the scaffold but "Statistically none of the findings was significant" [34]; Park et al. report perceived control with no effect sizes [4]; and METR's developers were 19% slower while believing they were 20% faster (T8). Every implication above is written as a test rather than a claim for that reason: measure the harness on task time against a prompt-only surface, with AI-on/AI-off randomised at the task level for the first cohort, before assuming any surface is the benefit.

## 2. Strand sections

### Strand 1 — The design-forward frontier (2023–present)

Ten sources from 2023 to 2026, weighted toward 2026 because the tools are. Delight clusters around channels that are not chat — anchored comments, sliders, direct file access, an editable semantics layer — and around import into the designer's existing tool; friction clusters around sameness, inconsistency across screens, metering, terminal-only setup and generators reproducing memorised real apps. Vendor and practitioner evidence disagree on fidelity, so several verdicts stay CONTESTED. "OpenDesign" resolved to nexu-io/open-design, now confirmed from its own README. *v2.1 (red team F1, F2, F12, F13):* the anchor product's standalone site was announced as closing on 2026-10-08, twelve days before v1 was built, and v2 never said so ([5], [57]); the incumbent shipped its own agent and its own write-to-canvas MCP (Figma Design Agent GA 2026-10-06, [56]); v0 shipped Design Mode (2026-06, [55]); Penpot shipped a hosted MCP with vendor-written rules for agent conduct on a canvas ([52]). The strand-1 agents of v1/v2 read launch posts and blog reactions and never opened a help-center notice, a changelog or a release page, so v2 was current to each vendor's marketing and stale to each vendor's product. The refresh entries [51]–[57] cover the last 180 days from changelogs, help-center notices and release pages; [6]'s third-party demo is demoted to a demo of scoping rules.

### [1]. v0: Generate UI with simple text prompts (Hacker News thread)
Hacker News commenters · 2023 · user testimony
Link: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37510699 — verified (via summariser; the HN page failed to load twice and was read through the hn.algolia.com export of the same item)
What it is: The 2023-09-14 announcement thread for Vercel's v0 (69 points, about 40 comments). v0 was a waitlisted private alpha at the time, so few commenters had used it hands-on. *v2.1 (F12):* v0 in 2026 is a different product — Design Mode with a floating toolbar, layers viewer and measure overlay (2026-06-08), an Apply step that regenerates code as a diffable version, Figma import (2026-07-31) and one-step GitHub publish (2026-09-01); that product is [55]. This entry stays as the privacy data point.
Delighted users: No hands-on delight evidence. The only positive signal is anticipation from a non-user: "For someone with no design chops I can't wait to start playing around." (jeremy_k)
Perturbed users: Access and trust friction — "A private option would be nice." (palmdeezy); it takes your input first and then asks you to register (mpixel); "You should make it clearer that this is a preview that people can't play with" (tptacek).
Verdict: HOLDS UP — the privacy and sign-up-after-input objections are about where the user's work goes, and that concern grows when an agent works unattended on their files.
Harness implication: Make the harness's first run (prompt to rendered screen) work with no account and no cloud round-trip for design files, and test it by checking that nothing but model calls leaves the machine.

### [2]. Inside Figma: a retrospective on Make Designs
Noah Levin (VP Design, Figma) · 2024 (July 17) · post-mortem (vendor-authored)
Link: https://www.figma.com/blog/inside-figma-a-retrospective-on-make-designs/ — verified (via summariser)
What it is: Figma's account of pulling its AI first-draft feature (Make Designs, later First Draft) days after Config 2024 after a weather-app prompt returned designs close to Apple's own app. Figma removed the assets, paused the feature and promised a better QA process. *v2.1 (F13):* the Figma story did not stop in 2024 — Figma announced the Design Agent on 2026-05-20, took it GA on 2026-10-06 and said design-system support would improve "in the coming months"; see [56].
Delighted users: No user reactions in the post; the only delight evidence is Figma's own pitch ("spin up a first draft of a UI design") — maker claim.

### Strand 3 — Low-code / no-code UXs of the recent past

Ten sources from 1987 to 2026: Atkinson's launch-year pitch, an ethnography of office spreadsheets, a FileMaker review, a FrontPage accessibility thread, two HyperCard post-mortems, Spolsky on the VB→.NET break, the VB6 petition, and two 2026 threads on leaving Bubble and outgrowing Retool. ### Strand 6 — Reconciliation, review and evaluation lineages (new in v3)

Eight sources from 1994 to 2026, numbered [58]–[65]. The red team's finding was that v2's five strands studied authoring surfaces while the recurring work in practice is reconciliation (design against build, human edits against agent edits), review (accept or reject, proposals) and evaluation (judging whether a design is good by an evaluator the agent cannot game); and that the field's best models for each already ship in tools designers do not think of as design tools. That is what this strand sources: track changes as the model for "another party edited my document while I was away" [60]; the pull request as the unit of unattended agent work, with its own critique of unattended noise [63]; Figma's published conflict rules for two parties on one design [61]; Interface Builder's separation of layout data from behaviour code [59]; the component catalogue with one story per state as a review substrate [64]; record-and-playback steered toward semantic locators [65]; Nielsen's heuristics as the evaluation lineage designers already trust [58]; and a market-level death with its exit friction documented by the vendor [62]. Two lineages were not reached despite 30 research opens: Dreamweaver's Roundtrip HTML (1997 — the WYSIWYG that did not rewrite code it did not touch, the counterexample to FrontPage [24]) and Abstract (design version control, sunset 2022–23) — both are the strongest candidates for FORGOTTEN BUT WORKS in the whole corpus and both stay untested; leads are in §4. Every entry here is a primary artifact or docs page; user testimony was not retrieved for any of them, which is the same finding as the strand-1 refresh — the mechanisms are documented, their reception is not.

### [66]. Response Times: The 3 Important Limits
